4 documented callbacks Directed by Angela Barnes Written by Lisa McQuillan 39 years after the original premiere

It's Homecoming weekend, bringing a number of Hillman alumni back to campus. Deborah becomes caught up in trying to secure a headlining performer for the Homecoming festivities. Dwayne and Whitley return to Hillman, but it becomes increasingly obvious that there are serious tensions in their marriage. Their problems begin affecting Deborah at the same time she's trying to manage her own increasing pressures.

Homecoming weekend brings alumni back to campus — the episode's whole engine.

S2·E29 — “A Stepping Stone” · first aired 1988-12-01
Whitley choreographs Gilbert Hall's step routine for a homecoming competition, gets thrown off the squad by captain Jaleesa for insulting "the rhythmless Freddie", and is put right by a meeting with her 92-year-old childhood idol.

Stepping, a homecoming competition and a dorm squad are HBCU ritual, not set dressing — the original treated them as the place where status gets negotiated. In the revival, homecoming is the excuse for the alumni (and the marriage trouble) to come back.

Dwayne and Whitley return to Hillman, and "it becomes increasingly obvious that there are serious tensions in their marriage".

S3·E49 — “Forever Hold Your Peace” · first aired 1989-11-02
Dwayne drives Whitley to New York for her father's wedding and gets a flat tyre in a blizzard with her. It is the original's pattern for them: two people with incompatible plans, forced into the same small space until something true gets said.

The original spent years making Dwayne and Whitley work for it. The revival opens their marriage under strain — which only means anything if you know the distance they already covered. That's the point of this page: the 2026 argument is the payoff of a fight you have to see to feel.

Deborah gets "caught up in trying to secure a headlining performer" for Homecoming.

S2·E27 — “Three Girls Three” · first aired 1988-11-17
Whitley and Jaleesa audition to sing backup for a Gladys Knight concert on campus; the original repeatedly hung episodes on a performer coming to Hillman.

A marquee name on an underfunded campus is a story about budget, prestige and who gets access to Black cultural capital — which is exactly the crisis the season ends on. The booker is never just a booker.

What the episode is about

Homecoming: alumni return, the institution performs its own continuity, and the marriage of the people at the centre of that history is visibly failing.

The original said it first — S5·E106 “Twelve Steps of Christmas” (1991-12-19)

By season five the original's core characters were married and negotiating holidays as families — the newly married Taylors blending traditions, Dwayne and Whitley circling — with class friction still intact.

What is happening now
  • Black alumni networks and HBCU homecomings are one of the few large-scale intergenerational Black institutions in American life; the wealth they concentrate and distribute is the subject of the racial-inequality literature. Source: context “racial-inequality”, 2026-10-04
  • Marriage and family formation among Black Americans happen in an economy with a persistent racial wealth gap — the difference between the original's 1990s couple and the 2026 couple is mostly the cost of the wedding and the mortgage. Source: context “racial-inequality”, 2026-10-04
Our reading — interpretation, not fact

Homecoming is where a Black institution displays its own continuity, which is exactly why the revival puts a failing marriage in the middle of it. The episode's politics are about what 'we made it' is supposed to buy — and how much of that promise is held together by one couple's willingness to keep performing it.
