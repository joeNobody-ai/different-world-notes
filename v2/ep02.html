The episode is titled "O Captain! My Captain!" — with Col. Taylor (Glynn Turman) recurring in the season as a military-titled professor.

S2·E23 — “Dr. War Is Hell” · first aired 1988-10-06
Col. Bradford Taylor arrives as the professor students call "Dr. War" — the calculus teacher Dwayne tries to avoid and then credits with saving him.

"O Captain! My Captain!" is Whitman on the death of Lincoln; it is also the line students shout for the teacher who changes them. Nobody has said the episode is about Col. Taylor — but the original's most feared professor is a captain, and he is in this season, and the title is about a captain being acknowledged. Treat this one as our bet, not a fact.

2

Deborah "gets herself into trouble after openly expressing her opinion and finds herself being called out publicly on campus".

S2·E32 — “Radio Free Hillman” · first aired 1989-01-05
Dwayne plays a bootleg rap record on his campus radio show; the dean pulls the show over the record's explicit content; Dwayne and the other students hold a sit-in at the administration building to protest censorship of student media. The dean does not punish the protesters. She suspends Dwayne from the station.

Thirty-seven years earlier the same confrontation — a student speaks, an institution answers, the student body organises — was the original's plot, and the original let the college be partly right. That is the harder, more interesting shape, and it is the shape the revival keeps reaching for.

3

Glynn Turman recurs as Col. Bradford Taylor.

S2·E23 — “Dr. War Is Hell” · first aired 1988-10-06
Col. Taylor is introduced as the professor whose standards terrify the students; he later marries Jaleesa Vinson — which is why the original ends with a Jaleesa Vinson-Taylor, and why "the aunties" in the revival are a married couple's worth of history.

The revival's older generation is not a cameo bench: these are people who, on screen, married each other, raised each other and buried each other's arguments. Their presence gives the kids something to be measured against.

S2·E41 — “Take This Job and Love It” · first aired 1989-03-16
Whitley damages Mr. Gaines' car, cannot bring herself to ask her father for the money, and takes a job at the Pit to pay for it — where she turns out to be good at the work.

The original understood that the money story on a Black campus is not a side plot; it decides who can afford to be a student. The revival's second episode puts a lead character's finances in the same slot.

Politics & the momentthe show's statements, and the original's
What the episode is about

A student expresses an opinion publicly, is publicly punished for it, and the student body's relationship to authority becomes the episode.

The original said it first — S2·E32 “Radio Free Hillman” (1989-01-05)

Dwayne plays a bootleg record on the campus station; the dean pulls his show for explicit content; students hold a sit-in at the administration building to protest censorship of student media. The dean does not punish the protesters — she suspends Dwayne.

What is happening now
  • Campus speech and DEI have been the sharpest institutional battleground of 2025–2026, with federal pressure on programs and curricula and courts still sorting out what stands. Source: context “dei”, 2026-10-04
  • Financial precarity shapes who can afford to take a public position: Rashida's money problem in the same episode as Deborah's public trouble is not a coincidence. Source: context “racial-inequality”, 2026-10-04
Our reading — interpretation, not fact

Nineteen eighty-nine gave this show its template and its discipline: the authority figure is not a cartoon, the punishment is real, and the campus keeps functioning afterwards. If the revival's second episode lets Deborah be simply right, it will have taken the easier and much less interesting road than the original took in 1989.
