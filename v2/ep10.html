The season finale is titled "A Gift & The Curse".

S1·E10 — “Gift of the Magi” · first aired 1987-12-17
Whitley learns her parents are divorcing and that her father has a younger girlfriend who is joining their holiday; she copes with Denise's help. Meanwhile the dorm's secret-Santa swap goes wrong because the gifts came from each other.

The original's tenth episode already knew that a gift from your family can arrive as damage — Whitley's holiday, her parents' marriage, her place in it. The revival's finale reaches back to that title for a season about a college whose survival, a marriage's survival and a student's future all arrive tied together.

S6·E122 — “Interior Desecration” · first aired 1992-10-08
By its sixth season the original had moved its characters into adult Hillman — Whitley and Dwayne sharing an apartment, Ron mediating, underclassmen stepping for unity — while the show itself was being wound down in 1993.

The original never got to tell us what Hillman looks like at fifty. The revival's tenth episode makes the institution itself the subject, which is the one thing a 2026 HBCU story cannot avoid: whether these places, and the students who need them, get funded.

3

Brandy performs the revival's theme song.

S2·E23 — “Dr. War Is Hell (theme era begins)” · first aired 1988-10-06
The original's theme song was co-written by Stu Gardner, Bill Cosby and Dawnn Lewis — who also played Jaleesa. Aretha Franklin sang the theme from seasons 2 to 5. Separately, and gloriously: Glynn Turman, who played Col. Bradford Taylor, was married to Aretha Franklin from 1978 to 1984.

A theme song is the cheapest, most effective generational handoff a revival has: it tells you whose record collection this show was raised on. The original's theme was written by a cast member and sung by Aretha Franklin; the revival's is sung by Brandy. That is the same move, thirty-nine years apart.

4

The finale "brings the various storylines together" and ends a season Netflix renewed within eight days of release.

S6·E141 — “A Rock, a River, a Lena” · first aired 1993-07-02
The original's final season is a handover season: the older cast graduating into adulthood, new underclassmen arriving, the show visibly changing shape in its last year before it ended in 1993.

Ending the first season with the college's future unresolved is exactly how the original behaved in its final year — it left the institution mid-sentence and trusted the audience to care. The revival has now been given a second season to say what happens next, which is a luxury the original's last year never had.

What the episode is about

The gala: the college's survival, the family's break, and the students' futures arrive together, with decisions made above the students' heads.

The original said it first — S4·E93 “Never Can Say Goodbye” (1991-04-25)

Dwayne and Whitley prepare to graduate and Ron discovers he cannot join them unless he passes a European history final — the original's consistent insistence that the degree, not the romance, is the thing at stake.

What is happening now
  • The institutions the fictional Hillman stands for are the subject of continuing federal funding and oversight fights, and they remain the most reliable ladder into the Black middle class. Source: context “hbcu”, 2026-10-04
  • Access to that ladder narrowed for the 2026 cohort after the 2023 Harvard decision, making the question of whether these colleges survive a question about who has anywhere else to go. Source: context “sffa”, 2026-10-04
  • Black maternal and infant health outcomes will be the lived experience behind the family decisions the finale sets in motion. Source: context “maternal”, 2026-10-04
Our reading — interpretation, not fact

A season that ends with an institution's future undecided is doing what a Black-college story has to do: refusing to pretend the ending is about individuals. The original spent six seasons treating Hillman as a real place with real bills, and got cancelled in 1993 with the question open. Season two of the revival now inherits that question, and this page's bet is that it will keep asking it.
