The episode is titled "Those Who Can...Cuff".

S1·E4 — “Those Who Can't...Tutor” · first aired 1987-10-22
Dwayne tutors Denise in calculus, gets locked in the dorm after curfew, climbs out of Whitley's window and takes the blame for it to protect Denise — costing Whitley her reputation for an episode.

One word swapped, thirty-nine years apart: 'those who can't teach, tutor' becomes 'those who can't commit, cuff.' The revival's titles are aimed at people who kept a worn-out VHS of the original — they are jokes about the joke.

"Cuffing season" arrives and the friend group's relationships get "considerably messier", with hooks-ups colliding with academia and money.

S2·E26 — “Dream Lover” · first aired 1988-11-03
The original's courtship was painfully slow and public: Dwayne notices Whitley at a party, she runs out of the dance, she dreams about him, she kisses him and then denies it, and Kim is delighted about all of it.

In 1988 the romance was a whole season of misread signals; in 2026 it is a phrase from an app. Same mess, different clock speed — and the revival is honest that its students date in a world with vocabulary the original's characters would have found alien.

Dates and hook-ups "intersect with academic pressure and financial problems".

S4·E73 — “Whitley's Last Supper” · first aired 1990-10-11
Whitley's father arrives and cuts up her credit cards, forcing her onto a budget; meanwhile Freddie and Matthew conspire to make Kim take a break from MCAT studying.

The original kept insisting that pre-med study and money are the same plot — you cannot be a student if you cannot pay, and you cannot rest if you are behind. The revival's fourth episode stacks the same three pressures in one week.

What the episode is about

Romance under financial and academic pressure; the college's social clock collides with who can afford to participate in it.

The original said it first — S2·E41 “Take This Job and Love It” (1989-03-16)

Whitley damages a car, cannot face asking her father for the money, and takes a job at the Pit — where she turns out to be good at it. Also in the same era: her father cutting up her credit cards and forcing her onto a budget.

What is happening now
  • Student debt and cost-of-living pressures fall hardest on Black students, whose families hold a fraction of the wealth available to White classmates; the 'who can afford a social life' question is a money question wearing a dating costume. Source: context “racial-inequality”, 2026-10-04
  • Consumer-facing Black economic protest in this decade has often taken the form of spending power — boycotts and blackouts aimed at corporations rather than institutions. Source: context “economic-blackout”, 2026-10-04
Our reading — interpretation, not fact

The original's dating episodes were always also class episodes: Whitley's problem was never the boy, it was the bill. The revival's 'cuffing season' episode is doing the same thing with a vocabulary borrowed from apps, and the money pressure in it is the load-bearing wall.
